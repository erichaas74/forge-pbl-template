The defining idea

One publication.
Many classes.
A lasting record.

Interviewing someone is the beginning. The defining product is an ongoing, student-produced public website with readers beyond the school.

Each class adds a new collection to the same online presence. Stories stay discoverable after the three-week block ends, building an archive of people, sports, events, organizations, and causes.

An athlete can share a profile. A grandparent can send a story to family. A local organization can point its community to your reporting.

Choose a reporting mission

Where will your story start?

Follow a question worth asking. Research the subject, make real-world contact with teacher support, and choose the format that serves the story.

01

Sports Reporting

Cover a real tournament, competition, or event. Research beforehand, interview participants, collect statistics and observations, and publish the coverage.

02

Beyond the Scoreboard

Explore an athlete’s training, setbacks, sacrifice, success, injury, travel, or experience balancing school and competition.

03

People Behind the Athlete

Profile a coach, trainer, physical therapist, official, parent, tournament director, or equipment specialist who makes athletics possible.

04

Inside My Sport

Research and interview someone interesting or accomplished in your sport: a college or professional athlete, respected coach, trainer, or sports journalist.

05

Voices of Our Community

Preserve a meaningful story from an older family or community member. Connect their personal experience to its larger historical time and place.

06

Community & Business Stories

Discover the people, problem, purpose, and impact behind a local business, nonprofit, entrepreneur, organization, or community leader.

From first question to public story

The reporting journey

Good reporting is a process. Each step makes the finished story more accurate, useful, and fair.

  1. Story assignment
  2. Background research
  3. Interview plan
  4. Real-world contact
  5. Reporting / interview
  6. Evidence & media
  7. Writing / production
  8. Editing & fact checking
  9. Publication
Outreach with teacher support

Develop the contact plan with your teacher, especially when approaching someone you do not know. Introduce the project, explain the intended public audience, and arrange an appropriate interview together.

Someone has trusted you with their story

Check quotes against your notes or recording. Verify facts, preserve context, secure appropriate media permission, and represent your subject fairly.

The format follows the story

More than an article.

A photo sequence, a recorded conversation, or a short documentary may tell the story best. Combine formats when they help your audience understand.

Written feature / profileVideo storyRecorded interviewPodcast / audio storyTournament / event reportPhoto storyData / statistics storyShort documentaryMultimedia combination

Every published story page carries author credits, sources, media, related stories, topic or sport tags, and a publication date. Captions and transcripts help more readers access the work.

The signature final · Publish Day

PUBLISH.

The new collection goes live on the public student site. Your work reaches the people whose stories you told—and readers you may never meet.

See a mock Publish Day edition ↗
EDITORIAL DESTINATION: READY TO PUBLISH
  1. Revise with an editor until the story is clear and fair.
  2. Confirm quotes, facts, sources, and media permissions.
  3. Complete credits, tags, captions, and the publication date.
  4. Receive Ready to Publish status, then join the class release.

A live launch can introduce subjects or premiere selected pieces. The final action is publishing the collection to its real audience.

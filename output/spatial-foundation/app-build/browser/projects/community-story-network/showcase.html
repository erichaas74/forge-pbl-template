Sample published story page · Written feature + media

The finish line was never the whole story.

Written by Alex Rivera · Student reporter
September 11, 2026 · Issue 03

PeopleTrack & fieldBeyond the Scoreboard

When the other runners started another lap, Maya Chen stayed at the edge of the track and opened her notebook. On the page was a goal that would never appear on a scoreboard: finish today’s planned session without comparing it with last year.

In this fictional profile, Chen is returning to a team routine after time away with an injury. Her account describes her own experience. The reporter does not turn that experience into medical advice or assume that every athlete’s return looks the same.

“I used to write down only my times. Now I write down what I learned at practice, too.”

Before interviewing Chen, the reporter reviewed the team’s public schedule and prepared questions about school, practice, and changing expectations. A follow-up conversation clarified that Chen had missed several meets, not an entire season—an important distinction in the final draft.

Chen described help from a teammate who checked in after practice and a coach who kept her involved when she was not racing. The reporter asked permission before using those details and checked the quote against the interview transcript.

The notebook does not prove how the next race will go. It records something more modest: what one student athlete decided to notice along the way.

Sources & reporting: Fictional interview with Maya Chen, September 7; fictional team schedule; follow-up fact check, September 9. These are illustrative source notes, not links to real evidence.

Media & credits: Typographic cover illustration. A real multimedia story could pair the feature with a permitted portrait and caption; no portrait or recording is included here.

Editorial record: Quote checked; missed-meet detail corrected; subject context and media permission reviewed. “Ready to Publish” is an illustrative status only.

Sample story page · Podcast / audio + transcript

A neighborhood remembered, one repair at a time.

Reporting and transcript by Jordan Ellis · Student reporter
September 11, 2026 · Issue 03

HistoryCommunityOral history

Elena Brooks recalls a repair shop that doubled as a neighborhood meeting place. The sample pairs personal memory with a plan for historical research.

Transcript excerpt

Jordan: What do you remember first when you think of the shop?

Elena: “The little bell above the door. You could hear it from the back room. Someone would bring in a lamp, then stay to ask how everybody was doing.”

Jordan: Do you remember when it closed?

Elena: “I think it was the summer after we moved. I would have to look at the old papers to be sure.”

Reporter’s narration: Her memory gives the shop a human voice, but does not establish a closing date. Before adding a date to the story, I would check town directories and local newspaper records.

Sources: Fictional oral-history interview, September 5. The closing date remains unverified and is not presented as fact.

Media & credits: Audio format mockup with a text excerpt; no recording supplied. The intended finished story would include permission to publish the recording, full transcript, and recording/editing credits.

Context: A person’s recollection is attributed as a memory. Historical documents can confirm, complicate, or leave parts of that memory unresolved.

Sample story page · Tournament report + statistics

What a tournament score can—and cannot—tell you.

Reporting and data by Sam Ortiz · Student reporter
September 11, 2026 · Issue 03

EventsBasketballData story

Riverbend won three games at the fictional Riverbend Cup, but the margin narrowed in each round. The results tell us how close the scores were. They do not tell us why the margin changed.

Invented tournament results for this mock showcase
RoundOpponentRiverbendOpponent scoreMargin
OpeningNorthside4236+6
SemifinalCedar3835+3
FinalWestfield4039+1

The margins were 6, 3, and 1 point. Their mean was 10 ÷ 3, or about 3.3 points. That describes these three games, not the whole season.

To explain what changed between rounds, a reporter would need play observations, reliable statistics, and interviews. A close final score alone does not prove that a team improved or that one player decided the game.

Sources & method: Fictional score sheets from the invented Riverbend Cup. Margins equal Riverbend’s score minus its opponent’s score; mean rounded to one decimal place.

Media & credits: Accessible results table prepared by the bylined reporter. No event photographs or recordings are included.

Editorial record: Results compared with sample score sheets; arithmetic checked; claims limited to the available evidence.

# Cultivated field

Original procedural geometry created by `docs/heist/build-coastal-field.mjs`. No external model was used. The six-crop GLB has 25 meshes and 130,784 triangles; 9,437,004 bytes. Offline merging preserves eight separately selectable target groups (six crops and two harvested samples). Separate AI-generated field backdrop and soil images are documented in `docs/heist/FIELD_IMAGE_ENVIRONMENT.md`.

The expanded field contains 80 maize plants, 80 sweet potato mounds, and 16 plants each of cassava, beans, chili peppers and squash. Six beds flank a central inspection path; harvested sweet potato roots and a maize ear remain accessible in the central clearing. Path-side maize plants retain individual kernel geometry; further plants use simpler ears to keep the complete model under the 150,000-triangle asset target. This is an offline geometry count, not a measured frame-rate result. The user confirmed that llamas should be left out to preserve the Hispaniola setting.

Depicts cassava with palmate leaves, maize with ears, sweet potato vines, bean bushes with trifoliate leaves and pods, chili shrubs with tapered fruits, and squash runners with lobed leaves and ribbed fruits. Wheat is intentionally absent from the historical reconstruction. The model is illustrative: separate bed layout, simultaneous maturity, spacing, varieties, colours, leaf shapes and harvest arrangement are not an archaeological reconstruction of a specific excavated field. No ordinary potatoes are claimed for this scene.

Historical references:
- National Park Service, [Taíno Settlement](https://www.nps.gov/places/taino-settlement.htm), identifies maize and sweet potatoes among Taíno crops. Salt River Bay, St. Croix, provides regional comparative context, not a direct field record from Hispaniola.
- Florida Museum, [En Bas Saline material remains](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/histarch/research/haiti/en-bas-saline/material-remains/), provides archaeological maize and manioc evidence from Haiti. This page is not used to claim sweet potato remains from that site.
- The same Florida Museum account identifies peppers and beans at En Bas Saline. USDA Forest Service's [regional historical synthesis](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-A13-PURL-gpo42485/pdf/GOVPUB-A13-PURL-gpo42485.pdf) lists squash among Taíno crops described by Spanish chroniclers. This supports regional crop selection, not this fictional plot's layout or specific varieties.

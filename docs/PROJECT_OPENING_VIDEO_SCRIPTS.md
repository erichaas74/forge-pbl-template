# Project opening trailers

Four deliberately overdramatic, student-facing trailers. Each previews the kind of finished work the student will create, then hands the unfinished project to them. These are scripts only; no launch content or existing interactions have been changed.

## Shared direction

- Aim for about 40–45 seconds per trailer. The five beats below can be generated separately as approximately 8-second clips; give longer spoken lines extra time in the edit.
- Treat an ordinary school project with the visual seriousness of a blockbuster: enormous reveals, dramatic lighting, close-ups, swelling music, and a sudden comic pause. The student should feel needed and capable.
- Show a glimpse of the future final product, then reveal its empty starting state. Never reveal the actual investigation answers or imply that a sample outcome is the student's result.
- Keep character appearance, clothing, locations, and voices consistent across clips. Use 16:9 for the main launch player.
- Record dialogue/voiceover as separate, editable audio if needed. Add readable titles, labels, project UI, and captions during editing rather than asking the video generator to render text.
- End with a brief musical resolution and a held frame for the real page's continue button. Do not put plan, goal, or path questions into the trailer. The future-chatbot preview follows the opening experience.

## 1. Frontier Trading Company — The Most Dramatic Budget Ever

**Tone:** A sweeping trading adventure interrupted by brutally practical mathematics. No guaranteed fortune; smart reasoning is the heroic achievement.

| Beat | Picture and sound | Spoken script |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · The fortune | Dawn. A trading street wakes. An empty wagon emerges through dust. A company founder places a modest purse on the desk as though unveiling a royal treasure. | **NARRATOR:** “A new trading season. An empty wagon. Two hundred dollars… and a reputation yet to be earned.” |
| 2 · The grand plan | A trader gestures toward towering crates. Camera cranes upward. The wagon gives an extremely unheroic squeak. Music cuts. | **TRADER:** “We buy EVERYTHING!” **BOOKKEEPER:** “With what money?” **[Beat.] TRADER:** “Perhaps… selected things.” |
| 3 · The pressure | Rapid close-ups: prices changing, cargo being measured, a muddy road, a loose wheel, the ledger reopening. Percussion builds. No battles or invented historical conquest. | **NARRATOR:** “Every crate takes space. Every journey costs money. And surprises rarely respect your budget.” |
| 4 · The future defense | Match-cut a wagon wheel into a circular graphic in an imagined company presentation. A season ledger, route, expense record, and explanation come together. Show comparison and revision, not a promised winning balance. | **NARRATOR:** “Build a company. Survive a season. Make the numbers—and your decisions—tell its story.” |
| 5 · The signature | A blank company charter lands on the desk. A pen rolls toward camera. Outside, the wagon waits in the morning light. | **BOOKKEEPER:** “Founder, the charter needs your name.” **TRADER, quietly:** “And possibly a smaller crate.” |

**End title:** FRONTIER TRADING COMPANY — MAKE EVERY DECISION COUNT.

## 2. Objects That Changed Us — The Museum Is Waiting

**Tone:** Awe, mystery, and a spectacular museum opening. Historical objects remain objects; the drama comes from revealing their context.

| Beat | Picture and sound | Spoken script |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · The silence | A dark museum corridor. A single footstep echoes. Lights wake one by one, leading to a closed exhibition wing. Low strings swell. | **NARRATOR:** “They crossed centuries. Outlasted generations. And now… they wait for someone to look closer.” |
| 2 · The objects | Slow, reverent camera moves around the supplied Nefertiti bust and coffin models. Cut to details of the supplied Horus temple model. Use the actual reference assets; preserve their form and markings. | **NARRATOR:** “A face shaped in stone. A carefully decorated coffin. A temple built to endure.” |
| 3 · The question | A curator stops in front of a dramatic display. The label is blank. The music awkwardly drops out. | **CURATOR:** “Magnificent.” **ASSISTANT:** “What will visitors learn?” **[Curator looks at the empty label.] CURATOR:** “We appear to be missing the important part.” |
| 4 · The future exhibition | Sweep through an imagined finished student wing: researched object labels, credited sources, an inviting museum board, and a recorded curator tour. Visitors lean in and compare details. Do not fabricate readable ancient inscriptions or unsupported interpretations. | **NARRATOR:** “Follow the sources. Build the connections. Turn a collection of objects into an exhibition people remember.” |
| 5 · The invitation | The imagined exhibition dissolves to the real empty wing. A spotlight settles on a blank entrance panel. The curator turns toward camera. | **CURATOR:** “The doors will open. The visitors will arrive. This wing needs your voice.” |

**End title:** OBJECTS THAT CHANGED US — OPEN A WINDOW INTO ANCIENT EGYPT.

## 3. History Live: The Revolutionary War — We Go Live in Five

**Tone:** Breathless modern breaking-news coverage of historical events. Deliberately use the project's modern-newsroom format; the presenters are fictional dramatizations.

| Beat | Picture and sound | Spoken script |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Breaking news | A dark control room erupts with light. Rival network desks fill the screens. A producer turns sharply toward camera. Urgent news sting. | **PRODUCER:** “Reports are coming in. The colonies are in turmoil. Both networks want the story. We need a reporter. Now.” |
| 2 · Competing headlines | Split screen. Two anchors deliver their lines with absolute certainty. Historical letters and reports appear behind them as editorial overlays. | **ANCHOR ONE:** “A stand for liberty!” **ANCHOR TWO:** “A challenge to lawful authority!” |
| 3 · The source check | A field reporter raises a microphone. The researcher slides a document into frame. The soundtrack halts on a record scratch. | **REPORTER:** “Sources say—” **RESEARCHER:** “Which sources?” **[Beat.] REPORTER:** “An excellent question.” |
| 4 · The future broadcast | Show an imagined student news package: studio introduction, field report, attributed quotation, map, and a clearly signaled correction. A producer checks the source list as the package reaches the studio monitor. | **NARRATOR:** “Find the evidence. Examine the perspective. Make a broadcast that earns its audience's trust.” |
| 5 · On air | A red tally light ignites above an empty presenter position. A microphone slides toward camera. The countdown appears in editing; leave a beat before the final word. | **PRODUCER:** “Your desk. Your report. Your name on the story. Stand by… you're on.” |

**End title:** HISTORY LIVE — MAKE THE HEADLINE. BACK IT UP.

## 4. Race Around the World — The Ocean Has Other Plans

**Tone:** A grand seafaring adventure whose captain discovers that a short line on a map is not a complete plan. Portray inhabited ports as communities with their own knowledge and interests.

| Beat | Picture and sound | Spoken script |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Departure | Harbor at dawn. Sails climb the masts. A chart unfurls across a table. The camera rushes from the map out onto a vast ocean. | **NARRATOR:** “Beyond the harbor: distance, uncertainty, and decisions no map can make for you.” |
| 2 · The shortcut | A confident officer draws an impressively straight line. A navigator glances toward darkening skies. A cook appears with a nearly empty biscuit tin. | **OFFICER:** “The shortest route. Brilliant.” **NAVIGATOR:** “Have you asked the wind?” **COOK:** “Or counted the dinners?” |
| 3 · The consequences | Headwinds strain the sails. Supplies are counted. A captain revises the chart. Cut to a port where crew and local traders listen and negotiate, rather than simply taking supplies. | **NARRATOR:** “Read the conditions. Weigh the cost. Every landfall brings other people's stories into yours.” |
| 4 · The future replay | A glowing route sweeps across an imagined final voyage replay. It pauses at decisions, alternatives, log entries, and source-supported reflections. The route changes as the student's explanation appears. | **NARRATOR:** “Record the choices. Reveal the consequences. Replay the journey—and explain why it unfolded that way.” |
| 5 · First command | The wheel stands empty. A navigator holds out the chart. The cook lifts the biscuit tin into view one final time. | **NAVIGATOR:** “Captain. Your first command.” **COOK:** “Preferably before lunch.” |

**End title:** RACE AROUND THE WORLD — YOUR ROUTE. YOUR REASONS.

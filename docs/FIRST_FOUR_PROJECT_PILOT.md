# First four projects for the AI tutor pilot

**Scope update:** Trading Post already covers math and Mystery Lab covers investigation. The current new-project work is limited to Expedition News Network and Hammurabi on Trial; use the [two-project eight-lesson plan](./TWO_PROJECT_EIGHT_LESSON_PILOT_PLAN.md). The selection below records the earlier proposal.

Select these four projects: one per subject and four different project types. They are drawn from the supplied grades 4–6 map and use the existing LMS type definitions. This is a pilot across grade levels; use each project's stated grade for its texts, tasks and assessment criteria.

| Subject / grade | Selected project | Project type | What students produce |
|---|---|---|---|
| ELA / 5 | **Expedition News Network** (`G5.ELA.U1`) | **Broadcast Studio / Live Newsroom** | Proposed pilot product: a sourced news report, revised script and short team broadcast, with individual writing evidence. |
| Math / 4 | **Explorer Route Math Quest** (`G4.MATH.U1`) | **Exploration / Voyage / Route Mission** | An Explorer Route Math Plan with route choices, supply calculations, measurement work and justified decisions. |
| Science / 4 | **Fossil Detectives: Rebuild the Lost Landscape** (`G4.SCI.U4`) | **Investigation / Mystery Lab** | A Lost Landscape Reconstruction Board with fossil evidence, an ordered rock-layer model, map evidence and an explanation of landscape change. |
| Social studies / 6 | **Hammurabi on Trial** (`G6.SS.U4`) | **Debate / Trial / Hearing** | A trial brief, cited law evidence, arguments/rebuttals, an individual defense and a reasoned verdict. |

These test four different ways of learning: producing a report, making route decisions, investigating evidence and defending an argument. Shared AI and teacher checkpoints can be tested across all four.

## ELA — Expedition News Network

**Why select it:** the newsroom gives reading, research, explanatory writing and revision a clear purpose. The map explicitly includes the shared revision process; its other literacy targets need detailed tasks before the pilot. The ELA map currently supplies a title and shared structure, so the final product above is a proposed pilot specification.

| Pilot target | Current map alignment | Evidence to require |
|---|---|---|
| FF.G5.ELA.12 — Explain evidence, central ideas, and relationships | Partial | Annotated source reading with accurate quotations, two or more central ideas, a summary and an explanation of relationships. |
| FF.G5.ELA.19 — Write informative explanations | Partial | Each student's organized explanatory report with facts/quotations, transitions, precise language and a conclusion. |
| FF.G5.ELA.21 — Plan, revise, edit, and publish coherent writing | Direct planned anchor | Draft, feedback, revised report and an individual revision memo. |
| FF.G5.ELA.22 — Research, evaluate sources, and use evidence | Partial | Source log, categorized notes, citations and an explanation of an evidence choice. |

**Pilot gate:** before a report moves to the team broadcast, the student must show that a central claim is supported by a source and explain the connection. Teacher sets the criteria; AI asks follow-up questions and identifies unsupported claims.

**Preparation:** choose grade-appropriate sources and a specific reporting question; define the reading, research and writing rubrics. Presentation and discussion remain useful activities, with additional standard credit only when their separate criteria are assessed.

## Math — Explorer Route Math Quest

**Why select it:** the map already explicitly connects whole-number operations, estimation, contextual remainders and measurement to the final route plan. It offers the strongest documented math match among these selections.

| Pilot target | Current map alignment | Evidence to require |
|---|---|---|
| FF.G4.MATH.03 — Solve multistep whole-number problems | Direct planned anchor | An individual logistics problem with equations, all required operations, remainder interpretation and a reasonableness check. |
| FF.G4.MATH.08 — Round whole numbers using place value | Direct planned anchor | Estimates with number-line justification, including the framework's rounding range. |
| FF.G4.MATH.11 — Divide multi-digit whole numbers | Direct planned anchor | Four-digit dividend / one-digit divisor problem, model and contextual explanation of the remainder. |
| FF.G4.MATH.20 — Solve measurement problems | Direct planned anchor | One- and two-step route/supply problems with appropriate units and calculations. |

**Pilot gate:** before committing a route or supply plan, each student explains a required calculation and what its units or remainder mean. AI gives a fresh equivalent problem; teacher reviews unresolved errors.

**Preparation:** set the route data, units, number ranges and decision constraints so the named standards are actually assessed. Addition and multiplication are practiced within the work; separate fluency standards require their own evidence.

## Science — Fossil Detectives: Rebuild the Lost Landscape

**Why select it:** it naturally fits the existing investigation type and has a coherent cluster of fossil, strata and landscape targets. The gaps can be addressed by adding specific investigation tasks within the existing mission.

| Pilot target | Current map alignment | Evidence to require |
|---|---|---|
| FF.G4.SCI.11 — Use fossils as evidence of the past | Direct planned anchor | Explain what a fossil is and how a fossil clue supports a past-organism or environment claim. |
| FF.G4.SCI.12 — Explain landscape change over time | Partial | A sequence and evidence explaining both erosion and deposition. |
| FF.G4.SCI.13 — Order rock strata with fossil evidence | Partial | An ordered rock-column model justified using fossil locations and layers. |
| FF.G4.SCI.14 — Investigate weathering and transport | Partial | Student-collected observations from a physical or interactive investigation, with analysis of material breakdown and movement. |

**Pilot gate:** before assembling the final landscape explanation, each student distinguishes an observation from an inference and justifies a proposed rock-layer order. AI probes the reasoning; teacher checks the evidence.

**Preparation:** supply a coherent fossil/rock/map case and add explicit deposition, relative-layer ordering and weathering/transport investigation activities. Preserve earlier explanations so changes in reasoning remain visible.

## Social studies — Hammurabi on Trial

**Why select it:** the named historical content fits a required grade 6 civilization topic, and the existing debate type supports evidence, opposing claims, questions and individual defense.

| Pilot target | Current map alignment | Evidence to require |
|---|---|---|
| FF.G6.SS.01 — Gather inquiry sources | Direct planned anchor | Varied primary/secondary sources with provenance and relevance notes. |
| FF.G6.SS.02 — Critically examine sources | Partial | Paraphrase and source analysis addressing purpose, viewpoint, bias, fact/opinion and argument strength. |
| FF.G6.SS.04 — Communicate evidence-based arguments | Direct planned anchor | An individual cited argument, response to an opposing view and evidence of civil discourse. |
| FF.G6.SS.10 — Evaluate government, society, religion, writing, and law | Partial; component focus | Evidence about Hammurabi's laws, authority and social hierarchy. Record the components demonstrated; the trial alone does not complete this bundled standard. |

**Pilot gate:** before entering the formal trial, each student explains a cited law in historical context and links it to a claim. AI challenges unsupported reasoning and offers a counterargument; teacher determines readiness.

**Preparation:** because this is unit 4 in the map, provide an introductory Mesopotamia/context packet and an entry check rather than assuming earlier units were completed. Full completion of FF.G6.SS.10 also requires its monarchy/empire, religion, cuneiform, architecture and epic-literature components; schedule those explicitly if full-standard completion is a pilot objective.

## Shared pilot structure and what to test

Use the same four-week structure in each project: week 1 launch and foundational evidence; week 2 individual application and team draft; week 3 checking, testing and revision; week 4 final individual check and group presentation. Each week has one instructional/individual session and one group session.

During both session types, AI checks each student's reasoning against a named Forge standard and rubric component. Team artifacts supplement individual evidence. The teacher defines thresholds, reviews difficult cases and confirms official completion. A stage unlock and a completed standard remain separate decisions.

Students needing support receive a short model, guided practice and a fresh independent check before the dependent task. They retain access to help and nondependent team work. Advanced students receive a new constraint, conflicting source, alternate explanation or transfer problem after demonstrating the required core skill.

For the first pilot, record whether individual evidence can be recovered from group work, whether AI feedback agrees with teacher judgment, whether gates hold only dependent work, whether retries capture improvement, and whether workload fits the sessions. These four projects do not claim complete annual subject coverage.

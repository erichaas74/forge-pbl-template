## Three approaches to discuss

1. **Meet someone at work.** Walk toward a working person, watch one short meaningful action, receive a greeting, then talk while remaining in that place. Closest to the user's original idea and the recommended base experience.
2. **Ask for a demonstration.** A question such as “Show me how” triggers a specific hand/tool/object action and a close inspection view. More valuable than an idle talking portrait; requires a verified animation or actual filmed/generated clip for each action.
3. **Follow part of their day.** Revisit the person preparing, working, and returning or sharing the result. Reveals family, community, weather, time and tradeoffs rather than turning each person into a dispenser of puzzle answers. More asset work; add after one successful encounter.

Recommended combination: start with meeting someone at work, plus one repeatable demonstration per person. Add the day sequence later.

## Encounter sketches

| Person | What the learner first sees | A useful demonstration | Life topics for the AI box |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crop farmer | Tending a planted mound, checking leaves, then placing harvested crops in a basket | Show a crop from growing plant to harvested part; compare cassava, maize and sweet potato | Work through the seasons; weather; who helps; sharing food; which task takes longest |
| Fisherman | Preparing fishing equipment beside the landing, checking a weight and sorting a catch | Show how one historically reviewed piece of equipment is used, then inspect it | Choosing a fishing place; weather; time away from the village; sharing the catch; learning the skill |
| Canoe builder | Working inside the dugout; chips visibly detach where the tool contacts wood | A short tool stroke changes the wood surface, followed by a close view of the hollowed single-trunk hull | Choosing a tree; learning from others; effort and helpers; caring for a canoe; what it carries |

These are proposed dramatized actions, not claims that exact actions, tools or labour divisions have been reconstructed. The roles are individual fictional people, not declarations of universal gender or social rules. Names, language, dress, equipment and action choreography need historical and cultural review before release.

## Student flow

Explore the world → select the person or use an accessible person list → approach → watch the brief task → person pauses and welcomes you → talk in the existing AI Tutor area → request a demonstration or inspect an object → optionally consult a linked source → Return to scene at the previous camera position.

The main panel stays visual. Questions and text input stay in the AI box. Captions, mute, replay and skip controls operate real media only. Reduced motion skips camera travel without skipping access. Students may leave at any time; no interview completion gate. An interview should not reset the camera, plant inspection or restoration draft.

## Preventing answer giveaways

- Characters describe their own work and experience. They never know the forged painting or identify which three parts to repair.
- The farmer explains what is grown when asked, rather than volunteering a list of absent crops. The canoe builder explains construction when asked, rather than opening with a statement about iron nails or screws.
- No future knowledge of European arrivals or Atlantic exchange is placed in characters' mouths. Modern historical comparison belongs in the source notebook/tutor.
- An explicit learner question can receive a relevant factual response; do not evasively withhold useful evidence. Observation, explanation and independent sources should support the student's conclusion.
- AI dialogue must be grounded in reviewed source material, admit limits, and distinguish dramatization from historical testimony. No AI service is connected in this change.

It supports research for the farmer/fisherman encounters, not every proposed gesture or net design. Use the existing canoe-construction and canoe-tools references for the builder. Do not substitute Calusa fishing equipment as direct Hispaniola evidence.

The current scene supplies plant/canoe inspection and an interview adapter boundary. It has no reviewed animated people, working/greeting clips, voice performances or connected AI interviews. Real filmed/generated video assets or suitable rigged characters with authored actions are needed. Camera shake, zoomed stills, placeholder playback and idle mannequins do not meet this requirement. Build one complete working → greeting → demonstration → return encounter for review before multiplying it across all three roles.

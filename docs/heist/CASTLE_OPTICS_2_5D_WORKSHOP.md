# Castle Rescue — Moon-tower optics

15 September 2026. The user requested the next lock after Fox. This pass owns **Lesson 5, Moon-tower optics only**.

## Interaction selected before implementation

A moonlit optical bench has two or three adjustable mirrors, brass angle rings, a light source, and a receiving lens. Drag the handle of a selected mirror; its reflected beam updates immediately while the angle snaps to the configured increment. Equivalent angle selectors and arrow buttons support touch and keyboard operation. Persist the completed drag, not every pointer movement. Grades 5–6 use two reflections; Grades 7–8 add a third mirror and a stone baffle.

We considered movable mirrors and a fixed-angle lens maze. Both would change the existing task. The chosen rotating-mirror bench preserves the authored geometry while making ray direction, obstruction, and reflection visible. A brief stable receiver contact engages the existing trial automatically. The receiver lever withdraws a pin and releases a counterweight, which lifts the owl grille. Two articulated owls react, unfold their feathered wings, fly through the fully clear doorway, and land on separate outside perches. Whole-scene, optical-bench, and owl focus views keep the activity readable on narrow screens.

```text

```

Keep exact rays, obstacle geometry, allowed angles, success conditions, award ownership, and saved drafts. No new dependencies, downloaded animal assets, deployment, or changes to other projects.

## Implemented

The optional `optics-cage` reflection presentation is enabled for Castle's four grade pathways. `optics-cage/` under the reusable Heist locks directory owns the bench, original articulated owl rigs, release timeline, native controls, camera, and presentation migration. The existing evaluator and `traceBeam` remain authoritative. The board-to-world transform preserves all angles and distances. Ray feedback updates during a drag, while one bounded input is committed on release. A stable solved arrangement starts one trial automatically.

The visible receiver cable runs to a sliding bolt and counterweight. The grille clears before either owl leaves. Feathered wings unfold, flap, and fold for landing; feet tuck during flight, and the waiting/landed birds blink and turn their heads. Replay is presentation-only. Pause freezes the sequence; reduced motion shows the open cage and both outside perches immediately. Saved solved arrangements restore the final pose without an extra trial or award. Keyboard/tap controls and the fallback renderer remain available.

Added assets are original procedural geometry in `optics-cage.model.ts` and `optics-cage.owls.ts`, plus shared 128 × 128 stone and wood maps generated by `diorama-surfaces.ts`. There are no new media downloads or package dependencies. GPU resources are disposed with the mounted viewer.

## Remaining weaknesses and next pass

The code-native owls are intentionally stylized, and the small portable material maps cannot provide close-up sculpted feather or stone detail. Focus views keep the entire mathematical mechanism usable on phones, where the whole-scene view is necessarily small. The highest-value next visual pass is a consistent animal/material art pass across the implemented workshops after user review; no additional lock was started.

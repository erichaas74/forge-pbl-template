# Two-project pilot: standards and eight-lesson plans

**Separate implementation plans:** [Expedition News Network](./EXPEDITION_NEWS_NETWORK_BUILD_PLAN.md) and [Hammurabi on Trial](./HAMMURABI_ON_TRIAL_BUILD_PLAN.md). Each includes its complete standards and eight lessons, plus graphics, student UI, reusable code, capability gaps and build acceptance criteria. Use those files for project-specific implementation; this file remains the shared curriculum overview.

The two new pilot projects are **Expedition News Network — Grade 5 ELA, Broadcast Studio / Live Newsroom** and **Hammurabi on Trial — Grade 6 Social Studies, Debate / Trial / Hearing**. Trading Post already covers the math pilot and Mystery Lab covers the investigation pilot.

Each project follows the new lesson sequence: **Launch → Plan → Evidence → First version → Revise → Rehearse → Defend → Final**. There are four weeks, with an instructional/individual session followed by a group session each week: **eight lessons per project**. These are two grade-specific courses, not sixteen lessons assigned to one class in four weeks.

The plans below define proposed curriculum and assessment. They do not describe completed LMS configuration or automatic mastery approval.

## Shared lesson and assessment approach

Plan around the standard first: choose the required content and individual evidence, teach the necessary skill, embed it in the project, and then define any prerequisite for the next task. A finished broadcast or a convincing trial performance cannot replace missing reading, source-analysis or content evidence.

**Workload assumption for the pilot:** 45–60 minutes per session. In an instructional lesson, allow about 10–15 minutes for modeling, 20–30 minutes for individual work and 5–10 minutes for checking/re-teaching. In a group lesson, allow about 30–40 minutes for collaboration and brief individual checks alongside it. Aim for one saved contribution or meaningful revision and one short independent check per student per lesson. This is a planning estimate, not a tested average or a completion quota.

**AI during live instruction:** after a student attempts the work, ask a short question tied to the current standard, identify a misconception, provide a hint or example during practice, and then give a fresh independent check. Save whether support was used.

**AI during group work:** ask every student about a specific contribution, source or decision. Rotate questions rather than accepting one spokesperson's answer for the team. Save the personal response beside the shared artifact. The teacher observes discussion and reviews the work; AI should not infer speaking/listening performance it cannot observe.

**Checkmarks:** track each student, Forge standard, required component, evidence, attempt and teacher decision. Use **Practicing → Evidence submitted → AI recommends ready → Teacher confirmed**. During a scored independent response, AI provides the prompt and agreed access supports, but withholds answer-generating hints. A stage gate unlocks a dependent task; it does not by itself complete a standard.

## Project 1 — Expedition News Network

**Mission:** report on a teacher-selected expedition so an audience understands its route, conditions and discoveries. Students distinguish scenario material from factual sources, research a focused question and produce an informative report.

**Final work:** a short team broadcast assembled from individual reports. Each student keeps a source log, reading analysis, report draft, revised report/script, revision memo and personal defense. For initial planning, use a roughly 250–400-word report per student and a 3–5-minute team broadcast; adjust length while retaining the assessed requirements. Every student presents a brief segment or a separate equivalent presentation.

## Project 2 — Hammurabi on Trial

**Mission:** decide whether the Code of Hammurabi created order, fairness, both or neither, using historical context and evidence. Students may reach different verdicts; the assessed outcome is the quality and accuracy of their reasoning.

**Final work:** a team hearing supported by individual source analyses, a Mesopotamia context record, a short trial brief, a rebuttal and a personal defense. A starting estimate is a 250–400-word individual brief and a short contribution to an 8–12-minute hearing; adjust for team size and access needs. Judges and clerks also submit an argument and answer an individual question.

### Standards to teach and check

| Label / Forge standard | Required learning and completion evidence | Lessons supplying evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **S01 — FF.G6.SS.01**, Gather inquiry sources | Collect relevant information from varied primary and secondary sources. Save source type, provenance, relevance notes and selected evidence. Use textual, artifact/visual and contextual materials. | L1–4, L7–8. |
| **S02 — FF.G6.SS.02**, Critically examine sources | Extract, summarize and paraphrase; distinguish fact/opinion; analyze purpose, viewpoint, bias, inference and argument strength. Save individual source annotations and critique. | L1–7; final evidence review at L8. |
| **S04 — FF.G6.SS.04**, Communicate evidence-based arguments | Cite evidence, compare viewpoints, explain cause/effect, predict outcomes, propose solutions and engage in civic discourse. Save the brief, response to an opposing view, proposed change with predicted consequences and hearing evidence. | L2, L4–8. |
| **S10 — FF.G6.SS.10**, Evaluate government, society, religion, writing, and law | Explain **monarchy, empire, hierarchy, polytheism, cuneiform, monumental architecture, epic literature and the Code of Hammurabi**. Maintain an eight-component context record with personal explanations and sources. | Content in L1–4; applied in L5–6; individual component review in L7–8. |

S10 is broader than the trial. This revised plan deliberately includes all eight components. Mark S10 complete only when the student's accumulated evidence demonstrates all eight; a strong law argument alone earns evidence for the law component.

**Materials to prepare:** a brief prerequisite/context introduction; a curated set of law excerpts with translation/source details; secondary context readings; attributed artifact/architecture images and an epic-literature excerpt; an eight-component context record; balanced case prompts; and source/argument/content rubrics. Historical claims and answer criteria must be grounded in these selected materials.

## Required gates, support and side quests

The teacher defines the observable criteria above before teaching. A gate is met when every listed prerequisite criterion is demonstrated independently. Missing criteria trigger support and a new equivalent check, rather than a generic percentage score. AI recommends readiness; teacher confirmation or a documented teacher decision controls the required progression.

Gates apply to the dependent part of the work. Keep teaching materials, scaffolds, practice, teacher help and nondependent team roles available. A team can continue its preparation while a student revisits a prerequisite; it cannot use another student's response as that student's proof.

| Project | Support when a student needs more time | Side quest after core readiness |
|---|---|---|
| Expedition News Network | Chunk a text, model an idea/detail distinction, provide a source-note or paragraph scaffold, then check a fresh passage without answer hints. Use accessible reading formats while retaining the target thinking. | Reconcile conflicting reports, justify a different visual, or adapt the explanation for a different audience and explain the changes. |
| Hammurabi on Trial | Use a vocabulary/context map, a shorter attributed excerpt and a modeled claim–evidence link; then ask the student to explain a new excerpt/case independently. | Compare another attributed law or viewpoint, expose a limitation in an argument, or predict an unintended effect of a proposed change. |

Both routes keep the same required standards. Extensions deepen or transfer learning; they do not substitute for missing core evidence or automatically approve acceleration. The supplied progression framework keeps English sequential.

## Pilot review and source basis

At the end, review actual time per lesson, checkpoint bottlenecks, quality of individual evidence during group work, AI/teacher agreement, success after re-teaching, and whether students can explain their final work independently. Use those observations to adjust the next run's pacing and prompts.

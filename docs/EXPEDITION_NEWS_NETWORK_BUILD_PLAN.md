# Expedition News Network — project build plan

**Local implementation available:** see [implementation, validation, and remaining gateway work](EXPEDITION_NEWS_NETWORK_IMPLEMENTATION.md). The project is registered at `/projects/expedition-news-network` with Endurance as its first expedition. The plan below remains the curriculum and classroom integration target.

**Model work added:** the launch (lesson 1) includes a 55-second narrated, captioned news piece. The final session (lesson 8, week 4) includes a 3:32 model broadcast with anchor/reporting sections, credited evidence, a route diagram, source limitations, and a conclusion. Both have transcripts and chapter links, remain viewable before checkpoints are reviewed, and are hidden during independent checks. Watching never completes a standard or replaces a learner's own portfolio.

**Grade / subject:** 5 ELA. **Project type:** Broadcast Studio / Live Newsroom.

This is the standalone curriculum, graphics, UI and implementation plan for this pilot. Build from **Launch → Plan → Evidence → First version → Revise → Rehearse → Defend → Final**: four weeks, one instructional/individual session and one group session per week, eight lessons total. The recommendations are planned work, not a claim of implemented or approved mastery tracking.

## Curriculum and final product

**Mission:** report on a teacher-selected expedition so an audience understands its route, conditions and discoveries. Students distinguish scenario material from factual sources, research a focused question and produce an informative report.

**Final work:** a short team broadcast assembled from individual reports. Each student keeps a source log, reading analysis, report draft, revised report/script, revision memo and personal defense. For initial planning, use a roughly 250–400-word report per student and a 3–5-minute team broadcast; adjust length while retaining the assessed requirements. Every student presents a brief segment or a separate equivalent presentation.

The original ELA map's three-week outline is expanded to this eight-lesson sequence.

## Lesson delivery, workload and AI assessment

Plan around the standard first: choose the required content and individual evidence, teach the necessary skill, embed it in the project, and then define any prerequisite for the next task. A finished broadcast or a convincing trial performance cannot replace missing reading, source-analysis or content evidence.

**Workload assumption for the pilot:** 45–60 minutes per session. In an instructional lesson, allow about 10–15 minutes for modeling, 20–30 minutes for individual work and 5–10 minutes for checking/re-teaching. In a group lesson, allow about 30–40 minutes for collaboration and brief individual checks alongside it. Aim for one saved contribution or meaningful revision and one short independent check per student per lesson. This is a planning estimate, not a tested average or a completion quota.

**AI during live instruction:** after a student attempts the work, ask a short question tied to the current standard, identify a misconception, provide a hint or example during practice, and then give a fresh independent check. Save whether support was used.

**AI during group work:** ask every student about a specific contribution, source or decision. Rotate questions rather than accepting one spokesperson's answer for the team. Save the personal response beside the shared artifact. The teacher observes discussion and reviews the work; AI should not infer speaking/listening performance it cannot observe.

**Checkmarks:** track each student, Forge standard, required component, evidence, attempt and teacher decision. Use **Practicing → Evidence submitted → AI recommends ready → Teacher confirmed**. During a scored independent response, AI provides the prompt and agreed access supports, but withholds answer-generating hints. A stage gate unlocks a dependent task; it does not by itself complete a standard.

## Required gates, remedial support and side quests

The teacher defines the observable criteria above before teaching. A gate is met when every listed prerequisite criterion is demonstrated independently. Missing criteria trigger support and a new equivalent check, rather than a generic percentage score. AI recommends readiness; teacher confirmation or a documented teacher decision controls the required progression.

Gates apply to the dependent part of the work. Keep teaching materials, scaffolds, practice, teacher help and nondependent team roles available. A team can continue its preparation while a student revisits a prerequisite; it cannot use another student's response as that student's proof.

| Project | Support when a student needs more time | Side quest after core readiness |
|---|---|---|
| Expedition News Network | Chunk a text, model an idea/detail distinction, provide a source-note or paragraph scaffold, then check a fresh passage without answer hints. Use accessible reading formats while retaining the target thinking. | Reconcile conflicting reports, justify a different visual, or adapt the explanation for a different audience and explain the changes. |

Keep the same required standards through both routes. Extensions deepen learning after core readiness; they do not automatically grant acceleration. The supplied progression framework keeps English sequential.

## Build boundaries and shared architecture

Keep curriculum, state, behavior and presentation separate, following `AGENTS.md`. Project names, sources, graphics, vocabulary, standards, lesson targets and rubric content belong in versioned configuration. Extend reusable components and registered capabilities when the current template cannot express a required behavior. Do not create a second application or project-named runtime services.

Use the repository's Angular standalone components, TypeScript contracts, signals and scoped services. Keep the existing project host and eight-lesson navigation as the entry point. Consolidate navigation into the existing LMS/project headers as the host evolves; do not stack another lesson bar, stage bar and permanent task banner above the work. Opening a lesson must preserve the mounted workspace or a saved draft.

The shared lesson model currently records `number`, `title`, `output`, `workspace`, `checkpoint` and `criteria`, with eight ordered lessons. Use those fields for the visible lesson plan. Keep Forge standard/component IDs, assessment attempts and gate dependencies in explicit typed assessment/rule configuration; do not parse human-readable `criteria` strings to award mastery. A lesson number, an internal workspace stage and a standard-completion state are different things.

The template registration currently uses a single-project-config package descriptor. Extra `lessons.json`, `assessments.json` or `rules.json` files are not automatically supported just because they exist. Either use the existing lesson registry and a validated configuration extension, or deliberately extend the loader/descriptor and version it. Add unknown-ID, reference, gate-reachability and final-submission validation with any such extension.

Maintain private responses and drafts per student; maintain the assembled product and agreed team decisions per team. Route changes through existing events, services and persistence adapters. Save text locally while typing, debounce persistence and flush on blur/navigation. Preserve before/after evidence and use retry-safe IDs for official submissions. Persist meaningful actions and completed trials, not animation frames or pointer motion.

The teacher and authoritative runtime confirm required gates, final submission and official standard completion. A disabled client button is only a UI affordance: validate the same prerequisite when the action is committed. AI feedback is advisory. Do not interpret a filled field, watched clip, saved draft, selected source or existing workflow check as mastery.

### Shared capabilities to resolve before a classroom pilot

**TEMPLATE_CAPABILITY_GAP — Standard evidence and tutor checkpoints**

- **Requested:** individual standard/component evidence, independent versus supported attempts, teacher decisions and prerequisite gates across all eight lessons.
- **Reason:** the inspected lesson contract defines teaching text and criteria; it does not itself implement this complete assessment workflow. Existing guide/help UI is not proof that a connected tutor exists.
- **Reusable approach:** a versioned assessment/evidence capability and tutor adapter that receive the current task, approved sources, rubric, permitted student/team evidence and attempt mode. Keep teacher-only criteria/answer keys out of student payloads. Feed concise summaries to the teacher review queue.

**TEMPLATE_CAPABILITY_GAP — Durable final media and class delivery**

- **Requested:** return to a recording after reload, share it with the correct class, and retry a failed upload/submission without losing or duplicating work.
- **Reason:** support differs between the existing templates and their adapters. Registered routes and a successful local preview do not establish end-to-end class delivery.
- **Reusable approach:** verify the current recording, asset-storage, authenticated session and submission adapters; extend only missing parts. Store media references/metadata in runtime state. Show “Saved on this device,” “Uploading,” “Saved for class” or a recoverable error according to the actual state.

## UI rules for young learners

Simplify the interaction while preserving the grade-level reading and reasoning. Use one question or educational operation at a time, with its source and answer area together. A decorative scene must never do the intellectual work for the student.

- **One dominant action:** a concrete verb such as “Save my note,” “Check my work” or “Send for review.” Keep secondary actions quieter. Opening a task starts with the child's work, not a dashboard.
- **Short instructions:** one sentence, then the source/task. Keep an optional “Show an example” or “Help me” control beside the problem. Do not put the entire rubric or all eight lessons in the center.
- **Readable controls:** use roughly 18px body text and 48–56px touch controls as initial design targets, with familiar labels and visible focus. Icons accompany words. Test these choices with students; these sizes are design choices, not claims about a compliance minimum.
- **Predictable navigation:** show the current lesson and one Lessons menu in the project header. Revisit prior work without resetting it. Use plain labels for My work, Team work and Help where needed; hide teacher controls from student view.
- **Focused help:** open one contained panel beside/above the current task. Move focus into it and return focus to the trigger on close. Do not cover the source passage while the child is answering about it.
- **Touch and keyboard:** provide tap/click and keyboard buttons for ordering/selecting items. Dragging may be optional; it must never be the only path.
- **Honest progress:** “Work saved,” “Ready for review,” and “Teacher checked” mean different things. Show a plain-language skill goal to the student; put exact Forge IDs and component details in the teacher view.
- **Accessible response paths:** captions/transcripts, labeled controls, text alternatives and teacher-approved accommodations must preserve the assessed construct. A speaking assessment still needs speaking evidence or an explicitly approved equivalent; typing alone cannot silently earn that credit.
- **Responsive layout:** at Chromebook/tablet widths keep source and response adjacent; at narrow widths stack source above response. Avoid horizontal page scrolling and fixed-height text clipping. Open details inside the visible workspace.
- **Calm feedback:** show one specific missing step and a retry action. Side quests appear as optional “Try a challenge” cards after core readiness. Avoid public student rankings or a visible speed contest for reading/reasoning.

### Graphics implementation rules

Use Angular HTML/CSS for controls, text, source cards and editors. Use authored WebP/AVIF images for scene atmosphere and inline SVG for maps, timelines, diagrams and simple emphasis. This is the recommended first-pilot implementation; the installed Phaser/Three.js packages do not need to load for these two reading/writing experiences. Consider a separate lazy-loaded scene plugin later only for a tested learning interaction that requires it.

Keep buttons and text out of flattened background images. Give SVG learning graphics a responsive `viewBox`, an accessible name/description and an equivalent text explanation; keep actionable choices as semantic HTML controls. See [MDN's inline SVG guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Guides/SVG_in_HTML).

Animate short visual feedback with CSS transforms/opacity after a meaningful state change. Use `prefers-reduced-motion` to remove nonessential motion; see [MDN's reduced-motion reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion). Never depend on an animation to communicate success or completion.

Use responsive image sources, explicit dimensions and useful alt text; mark purely decorative art as decorative. Load the current scene first and defer later scenes/media. Pause unused media and clean up streams, listeners and object URLs when appropriate. Recording support must be feature-detected, including supported formats where needed; [MDN documents `MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported()`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaRecorder/isTypeSupported_static). An unsupported or failed recording must offer a supported teacher-approved path without manufacturing evidence.

Illustrations and scripted characters are engagement assets. Historical quotations, images used as evidence, charts and claims need attribution and clear source identity. AI-created decorative art must not be presented as an authentic source.

## Student screens and graphics — Expedition News Network

Use a friendly field-newsroom setting: a desk, a folded expedition map and a media screen. Keep the central paper/screen area quiet enough for real reading and writing. Opening art introduces the mission; the reading or reporting task takes over the center as soon as work begins.

```text
LMS header:      Projects                       Learner / access tools
Project header: Expedition News   Lesson 3 of 8 ▾   Saved   Help
--------------------------------------------------------------------
             Find evidence for your report
  [Source passage / map]     [My note and why it matters]
                            [Save my note]
--------------------------------------------------------------------
```

This is the reading-workspace layout, not another permanent instructional banner. On a small screen, show the passage above the note; the main action stays beside the note. Keep article length and grade-level complexity intact even when the controls are simple.

| Lesson | Main screen | Main action / learning visual |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Launch | One anchor article with an annotation response | “Save my ideas”; student-selected detail highlights. |
| L2 Plan | One reporting question and the team's short plan | “Add my part”; labeled role cards and a simple story outline. |
| L3 Evidence | Source reader beside personal notes | “Save my note”; map/diagram and source identity remain visible. |
| L4 First version | Individual report with its evidence links; team rundown on request | “Check my report”; a short ordered list of segments, not a video-editing timeline. |
| L5 Revise | Earlier and current writing, with one focus | “Save my revision”; simple before/after emphasis linked to the student's explanation. |
| L6 Rehearse | Report script and one preview screen | “Start practice”; one purposeful map, image or quote card. |
| L7 Defend | One independent prompt, then the portfolio review | “Send my answer”; clear practice/check mode, no answer hints during the check. |
| L8 Final | Team broadcast player and the student's contribution | “Present my report”; simple title card, readable captions and source credit. |

### Graphics and interaction choices

| Element | How to code it | Simplification |
|---|---|---|
| Newsroom art | One responsive illustrated image behind/around the semantic workspace. Separate close-up art only if it improves orientation. | No free camera, avatar movement or hotspot hunt before reading. |
| Expedition map | Inline SVG paths, a few labeled places and configuration-backed source references. Fit with `viewBox`; use a selected-place caption. | Selecting a place opens its source beside the map. A map never auto-writes the student's conclusion. |
| Story options | Two or three illustrated HTML buttons chosen by the teacher; one can be preassigned for the youngest/newest users. | Start with a question, not networks, formats, camera presets and story genres at once. |
| Evidence notes | HTML passage, quote selection, note field and source chip. Bind selection to a stable source/passage ID. | Default to the current source; open the larger library only on request. |
| Report/script | A normal text editor with a small number of sections and source links. Reuse script blocks under a simpler presentation. | Student labels such as “My opening,” “Explain,” and “My ending”; advanced production block names stay out of the default view. |
| Broadcast preview | Reuse the existing player/evidence-scene components and a single approved visual preset; add readable HTML titles/captions. | Start with image/map plus student narration. Camera video is optional when the teacher's assessment permits it. |
| Save/review feedback | Inline text and icon after the actual save/review response. | A checkmark does not mean that a claim has been fact-checked or a standard mastered. |

A visually polished first pilot needs only a newsroom background, a small set of source/story thumbnails, one map or diagram asset, a title-card style and a few labeled icons. Reuse the same visual vocabulary across all eight lessons. Source material remains separately readable and attributable.

### Data and checks to wire

- The project config owns the reporting question, vetted sources, story choices, illustration assets and six standard mappings. Each lesson points to the relevant task and gate criteria.
- Student records own reading answers, notes, citations, draft history, revision memos, independent checks and teacher decisions. Team records own the agreed plan, rundown and assembled broadcast.
- E-A is checked before dependent report drafting, E-B before recording/rehearsal and E-C before assessed final release. Help, reading and nondependent contributions stay available.
- AI requests include the selected source/passage, the student's actual note/report and one target criterion. Practice can suggest a next step; an independent check prompts and records the student's reasoning without composing it.
- Show only one tutor prompt at a time. Use wording such as “Which sentence supports your idea?” Keep detailed standard codes and evidence histories in the teacher view.

### Build sequence and acceptance

1. **Curriculum/config:** author the expedition question, source set, rubrics, six standards and eight lessons. Resolve the generic-newsroom profile and register any necessary reusable extension. Keep the legacy broadcast project working.
2. **First complete slice:** implement L1–L2 with the focused article/note screen, save/resume and teacher-reviewed E-A. A student must be able to explain why their source helps.
3. **Reporting loop:** connect L3–L5 to individual notes, evidence-linked reports, revision history and E-B. Then add the lightweight map/background graphics to the working task.
4. **Showcase:** connect L6–L8 to rehearsal, independent checks, E-C, individual presentation evidence and the assembled broadcast. Verify media persistence and submission retries.
5. **Pilot checks:** verify each rubric component against saved personal evidence; no group broadcast auto-completes everyone's standards. Test gate failure → support → fresh check → teacher release, keyboard/touch operation, 200% zoom, narrow layouts, reduced motion, microphone denial, failed upload and resume without draft loss. Check existing broadcast configurations after the generic-model change.

Observe the first few students starting L1 and finding their saved work without navigation coaching. Use their behavior to reduce controls or clarify labels before adding more visual effects. No application code or artwork is created by this planning document.

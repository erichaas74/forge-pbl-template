# Student resource ideas for both debates

Shortlist checked 2026-09-15. These are proposed resources and teaching uses, not newly embedded media. Use short selected passages for Grade 6; full scholarly translations are reference material. Keep the main viewport on the debate and open resources on demand.

## Best starter set

| Resource | Best use | Student action |

| [ESU — Listening and Response](https://www.esu.org/resources/skill-sets-listening-and-response/) | Sessions 2–6: selecting an important opposing argument and giving a focused rebuttal. | Summarize the opponent fairly, then select the claim your response will answer. |

## Useful supporting resources

- [The Met — The Roman Republic](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-roman-republic): contextual reading for institutions and the late Republic. Use a short section to build a timeline or explain what was at stake in concentrating authority. Treat it as a modern museum explanation.
- [Plutarch — Caesar, Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Plutarch/caesar.html): a challenging extension in translated ancient biography. Select a brief passage; compare the author's portrayal with another source. Explain that this is a later account, not a transcript of Caesar's speeches or an eyewitness record of every event.
- [British Museum — How to write cuneiform](https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/how-write-cuneiform): an illustrated explanation and curator-video candidate. Ask how writing helps preserve law and literature. Distinguish a writing system from a language and a later tablet from Hammurabi's own period.
- [ESU — Educator and judge resources](https://www.esuus.org/what-we-do/middle-school-debate-public-speaking/resources-for-educators-and-judges/): middle-school debate materials for adapting a short student judge guide. Use the project's visible rubric consistently; do not introduce a competing scoring system halfway through the debate.

## Resources we should author inside the project

These are design ideas rather than claims about external resources:

1. **Two versions of one argument.** Same claim and source, with a weak and improved explanation. Students choose the stronger version and identify the changed reasoning. The current build already includes this kind of interactive skill comparison.
2. **Rebuttal pairs.** An opponent makes a claim; students choose between a personal attack, an unrelated fact and a direct evidence-based response. Show why only the last answers the argument.
3. **Annotated student speech models.** Clearly fictional or used with permission. Mark claim, source, concession and rebuttal; offer a transcript and actual playback when media exists.
4. **A source detective card.** Who made this? When? For whom? What can it establish? What remains uncertain? Keep the written response in the tutor area.
5. **A peer-feedback example.** Compare “Good job” with a specific observation, exact speech moment and useful next step. Have students apply the same pattern to another person on their side.
6. **A judge calibration round.** Students rank three short performances and compare the evidence behind their decisions. Keep ties possible; reward supported judgments rather than agreement with a favored side.

## How the resource shelf should work

- Use four short filters: **Evidence**, **Build an argument**, **Watch a speech**, **Critique and judge**.
- Each item needs a visible source/author, a short purpose and one concrete action. Offer the original source beside a clearly labeled student adaptation.
- Suggest one or two resources for the current lesson, while keeping all resources available during testing.
- After viewing, return to the selected debate argument. The useful outcome is a better response or a more specific critique, not a “watched” checkmark.
- Famous speeches can model rhetoric and delivery. Use the project's historical sources to support historical claims.
- Before adding a video player, verify the actual clip, captions/transcript, classroom access and rights. Candidate links above have been checked as resources; no new video file or embedded player has been delivered.

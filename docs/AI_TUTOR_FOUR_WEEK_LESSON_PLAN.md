# AI tutor: four-week project lesson plan

Use one four-week cycle for each selected project: one instructional/individual session followed by one group session each week. This is eight lessons per project, not a schedule for completing the whole catalog in four weeks. Keep activity pacing flexible within these checkpoints.

The final product starts in Week 1 and grows from the work saved throughout the course. Each individual lesson prepares something students bring to their group. Each group lesson ends with a shared checkpoint and a brief contribution or explanation from every student. For individual projects, groups serve as feedback and playtesting teams while students retain their own final products.

This is a proposed teaching and AI integration plan, grounded in the current project catalog, project configurations, and available workspaces. It does not describe an already connected course-wide AI tutor.

**The eight-lesson flow**

| Week / lesson | Session and basic checkpoint | Work and evidence saved | Teacher tasks | AI tutor tasks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 / L1 | Instructional/individual: understand the challenge and try the first skill. | Initial idea or prediction, one practice attempt, and a question. | Introduce the project and final outcome; model a core skill; notice starting needs. | Ask the student to explain their attempt; clarify a concept; suggest a first step. |
| 1 / L2 | Group: agree on a direction and begin the project. | Shared plan or outline, roles, initial evidence, and each student's contribution. | Support group formation; check that the plan is manageable and connected to the goal. | Prompt comparison of ideas and reasons; help the group identify what it needs to find out. |
| 2 / L3 | Instructional/individual: develop the next skill and gather useful evidence. | A source note, calculation, test record, or draft section, with a short explanation. | Teach the next needed concept; address misconceptions from Week 1. | Give targeted practice or a hint; ask how the evidence supports the student's thinking. |
| 2 / L4 | Group: combine work and produce a first version. | First working product or completed trial, supporting evidence, and an unresolved question. | Check progress and evidence quality; help teams choose what to improve. | Point to gaps in the group's explanation; ask members to compare results before deciding. |
| 3 / L5 | Instructional/individual: examine feedback and improve a contribution. | A revised explanation, calculation, design, or draft, with a brief reason for the change. | Model revision or a more advanced skill; hold short student conferences. | Compare the student's earlier and current work; ask a focused question that supports revision. |
| 3 / L6 | Group: test, challenge, or rehearse the near-final project. | Near-final version, peer feedback or test results, and a short improvement list. | Facilitate critique, playtesting, or rehearsal; check everyone's participation. | Prompt a counterargument, comparison, or further test; help organize next steps. |
| 4 / L7 | Instructional/individual: show understanding and prepare a final contribution. | An individual explanation or small transfer task, selected evidence, and a final revision. | Check individual understanding; give focused reteaching and presentation guidance. | Coach rehearsal and evidence selection, then leave the student to give their own explanation. |
| 4 / L8 | Group: complete the final challenge, presentation, or publication. | Final product or performance record, selected process evidence, and each student's short defense/reflection. | Run the final event; ask questions; assess the product and individual learning. | Support preparation and navigation; afterward prompt reflection and summarize progress for teacher review. |

**How the tutor is embedded**

- Place tutor access beside the current activity: evidence board, source reader, lab, ledger, code editor, design notebook, or draft. It should know the lesson goal, relevant project materials, and the student's current and earlier work.
- Use a short cycle: student attempts the work → tutor asks or hints → student responds or revises → save the checkpoint. Questions should point back to actual evidence in the project.
- During individual sessions, tailor help to the student's understanding. During group sessions, support discussion of shared work and prompt each member to explain a contribution.
- Carry each checkpoint into the next lesson and the final product. Preserve the original and revised work when a change shows learning. A brief note about useful tutor feedback can accompany the work; chat volume is not evidence of mastery.
- Give the teacher a short view of completed checkpoints, useful evidence, and students who may need help. The teacher decides pacing, readiness, feedback, and assessment; the tutor provides advice without supplying final answers or writing the product for students.

The following project maps specify the basic work to save at all eight lessons. The teacher and tutor tasks in the shared table apply to every map. A checkpoint can be a few sentences, an annotated image, a calculation, a saved trial, or a short recording; it does not need a separate worksheet.

**What needs to be added to support this plan**

Start with a shared eight-checkpoint lesson view and links to each project's existing work. Add a tutor that can refer to that work, preserve useful feedback and revisions, and give teachers a brief progress summary. Pilot the flow with The Unlabeled Shelf and one contrasting project such as Survival Island Story Lab before extending it across the catalog.

Current project maturity varies. Journey Replay already has a bounded reasoning-coach contract, and Narrative Studio has a replaceable coach with a scripted local preview. Neither establishes a connected tutor across all projects. Some final-event records and teacher review steps proposed above will need to be added or collected by the teacher. Cascade Bay currently provides the response loop without a final-submission/showcase workflow; Community Story Network currently provides an introduction and mock showcase, with publication intended for a separate host. The homepage's unbuilt race-car engineering and research-symposium concepts are outside these sixteen project maps.

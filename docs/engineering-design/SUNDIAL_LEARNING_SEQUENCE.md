# From Sundial to Sun Monument

The Grade 5 Preview now starts with a vertical-post sundial. The 3D shadow canvas remains the work surface throughout five ordered lessons. Instructions and notes expand above it; the creative sample library and full monument builder appear in the last lesson.

1. **Build a sundial.** Choose a place and a 10–200 cm post matching the classroom model. Play sunrise to sunset, pause, and mark the calculated shadow tip. Morning, solar-noon and afternoon shortcuts help students get started. Up to eight time marks retain their original date, place, time and post height. Repeat the day to watch the shadow revisit them.
2. **A seasonal surprise.** The lab switches to a contrasting seasonal date while preserving the same post and marks. Students compare June and December at solar noon and play each day. The prompt asks why the tip misses the old marks before the tilt explanation is introduced. General research and Earth-explanation controls are withheld in the first two steps to preserve that observation-first sequence; this is pacing, not a security gate.
3. **Why the shadows change.** The synchronized Earth view opens, showing rotation, a fixed tilted axis, orbit position and the selected site's sunlight. Students revise their first explanation in a separate saved response.
4. **Make a Sun calendar.** Students mark both equinoxes and both solstices at local solar noon. These four marks fit alongside the eight daily marks. Nearby-date controls compare a week before/after the selected special date. Numbered badges and a legend separate nearby points, including the equinox pair. Recorded practice tests report distance from each mark to the current shadow tip, rather than treating any shadow covering a mark as success.
5. **Build a Sun monument.** Students apply those discoveries to a creative design using the existing stone shapes, bores, colored glass/jewels and central sculpture. Sample previews, the full builder and the four-date final demonstration are available here. The earlier practice dial stays saved independently.

Students can revisit any step; moving ahead does not claim that an answer is correct or an assessment is complete. Research answers save on blur, and the first hypothesis and revised explanation use separate research IDs. Design changes and lesson selection save through the existing scoped runtime. Individual trials can be replayed in their originating workspace. Both designs and the learning notes are included in notebook export.

## Science and practical limits

The introductory instrument uses the ground projection of the **top centre of a vertical post**, on a level plane. For the vector toward the Sun, its tip is `(post.x − height × sun.x / sun.y, post.z − height × sun.z / sun.y)`. The post's finite width also creates a full shadow footprint. The small moving ring identifies the reference tip; numbered marks stay at their measured positions. Camera changes never move these points. Near sunrise/sunset the tip may be beyond the ±12 m marking court; marking explains that limit instead of clamping the physical projection.

This is a simple shadow-tip dial, not a claim that every sundial loses its hour lines when seasons change. A pointer parallel to Earth's axis can retain useful hour lines throughout the year. Clock time also differs from solar time; solar-noon comparisons isolate seasonal altitude changes from clock conventions. Sources: [NASA sundial explanation](https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/Sdial1.htm), [Royal Museums Greenwich sundials](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/time/sundials), and [NASA's vertical-post sundial activity](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Create-A-Sundial-Activity.pdf).

March and September produce similar noon marks. Solstice changes are slow near the extreme, so a single matching point does not establish a unique date. Tropical sites can have their highest noon Sun on dates other than the solstices. Seasonal dates retain the existing 2025–2030 event table and local-date conversion. Direct geometric sunlight, level terrain, and illustrative colored transmission remain the existing model assumptions; refraction through focusing lenses, weather, penumbra and structural stability are not added here.

## Reusable implementation and compatibility

- `EngineeringDesignConfig.learningSequence` optionally provides one practice design and up to ten steps. Each step has content, an installed simulation activity ID, a practice/project workspace, optional research prompt/explanation, and optional guide visibility. Validation rejects duplicate steps, unknown research references, invalid designs, and sequences without a final project step. Projects without the option retain their previous input contract.
- Optional snapshot fields `practiceDesign` and `learningStepId` preserve old saved designs and existing `1.0` envelopes. `engineering.learningStepSelected` is registered through the runtime event registry. The existing measured-design event stores edits in the selected workspace. Final predictions are not copied into unrelated practice trials.
- The optional `DESIGN_CHANGE` callback carries validated measured designs from an installed simulation through the template runtime. Frame messages require exact source, origin, channel and current editable activity. Read-only previews cannot write. Changing lessons cancels pending receipts, and evidence restore occurs after lesson initialization so its observation date wins.
- Target `settings` is a bounded optional dictionary (eight short string/finite-number fields). Solar-specific marker meanings stay in `sundial-lab.js`; both host and optical-solver validators check its shape. Captures add workspace/lesson tags within the existing twenty-field settings limit. No project-name branches, new dependencies, core schema changes, or backend write path were added.
- The local package remains `calendar-monument@1.0.0`, already marked **Preview**, so current browser drafts continue to load. Any eventual published release must use the normal immutable version-publishing workflow. No deployment or authoritative submission is part of this change.

Files: the new curriculum sequence and updated opening/catalog/config; generic engineering models, runtime, guide/page and callback contracts; the solar Angular bridge; `sundial-lab.js`, the static controller, markup, styles and mirrored target validator; focused Angular and Node tests. The original `grade-5/Globe-Sun-Shadow` source is unchanged.
